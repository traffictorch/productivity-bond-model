📖 What is the NPI?▼

The National Productivity Index (NPI) measures how efficiently an economy converts its key inputs into economic output. It is defined as:

NPI = GDP ÷ (Labour0.40 × Energy0.25 × Materials0.20 × Capital0.15)

Higher NPI means the economy produces more value from the same inputs – a sign of genuine progress, not just growth.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions▼
What is the NPI?
The National Productivity Index measures how efficiently an economy converts inputs (labour, energy, materials, capital) into GDP. Higher NPI = more efficient.
What are the weight modes?
Book NPI uses fixed weights (40/25/20/15). Country Preset applies weights based on the country's economic structure. Custom lets you adjust freely.
Where does the data come from?
GDP (World Bank), Hours (ILO/OECD), Energy (IEA), Materials (UNEP), Capital (OECD/Penn World Table). All public sources.
Why is some data missing?
Not all countries report all metrics. The tool skips years with incomplete data and shows a warning.
