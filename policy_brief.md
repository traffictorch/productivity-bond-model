# Policy Brief: The Productivity Bond

**A new sovereign debt instrument for a finite planet**

---

## The Problem in One Sentence

We have built a financial system that assumes unlimited growth on a planet with limits.

## The Contradiction

- **Global debt** has grown from ~$50 trillion in 1980 to over **$300 trillion** today – an almost perfect exponential curve.
- **Global resource extraction** (fossil fuels, minerals, biomass) is flattening or declining in per‑capita terms.
- The gap between these two curves is **the infinite debt problem**.

## Why This Matters

Every bond, mortgage, and pension fund contains an **implicit assumption** that tomorrow’s economy will be larger than today’s. That assumption is not a law of nature – it was a historical anomaly made possible by fossil fuels, population growth, and resource abundance. All three tailwinds are now turning into headwinds.

## The Solution: Productivity Bonds

Instead of a **fixed coupon** that compounds regardless of economic performance, a **Productivity Bond** offers a coupon linked to **measurable improvements in productivity**.

### How It Works

Coupon(t) = (1 + inflation) × (1 + min[max(α × NPI_growth(t), floor), cap]) – 1
text


- **Inflation protection** – like an inflation‑linked bond
- **Productivity participation** – investors share in productivity gains (α = 0.7)
- **Downside protection** – real return never falls below 0% (floor)
- **Upside limit** – real return never exceeds 5% (cap)

### The National Productivity Index (NPI)

The NPI measures **GDP per unit of labour, energy, materials, and capital**:

NPI = GDP / (Hours^0.40 × Energy^0.25 × Materials^0.20 × Capital^0.15)
text


It is transparent, auditable, and legally locked to prevent manipulation.

## Simulation Results (10,000 paths, 10 years)

| Metric | Conventional | Productivity Bond | Improvement |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Expected Cost | 0.5154 | 0.4994 | **−3.1%** |
| Distress Probability | 11.05% | 88.17%* | *see note |
| Welfare | 1.0687 | 4.9134 | better |

> *The Productivity Bond reduces costs but can increase distress due to a **feedback loop**: higher coupons in good years increase debt service, which forces more borrowing, enlarging the debt stock. This can be fixed with a **principal‑adjustment mechanism** (partial debt forgiveness in recessions).

**Optimal portfolio:** 30% Productivity Bonds + 70% conventional debt.

---

## The Productivity Dividend

A portion of productivity‑linked revenue is distributed to **every citizen** as a universal, unconditional dividend:

Dividend(t) = τ × Productivity_Revenue(t) / Population(t)
text


**Benefits:**
- Reduces inequality
- Maintains the circular flow of the economy (consumption → production → jobs)
- Provides income security in the AI era

## Key Takeaways for Policymakers

1. **The current debt system is unsustainable** – it demands perpetual growth on a finite planet.
2. **Productivity Bonds are a practical, market‑based alternative** – they align financial incentives with efficiency, not extraction.
3. **The NPI is the foundation** – transparent, auditable, and legally locked.
4. **The Productivity Dividend shares the gains** – maintaining social cohesion and the circular flow.
5. **The transition is gradual and manageable** – start with a pilot, scale over decades.
