# Adoption Scenarios for Productivity Bonds

**Practical pathways for different country types**

---

## Overview

Not every country faces the same constraints or has the same institutional capacity. This document outlines three realistic adoption scenarios, ranging from advanced economies with high debt to emerging economies with growth potential to small open economies with strong institutions.

Each scenario includes:
- A country profile
- A phased pathway (pilot → expansion → integration → transformation)
- Key enablers
- Key risks

---

## Scenario A: Advanced Economy with High Debt

**Example countries:** Japan, Italy, United States

**Profile:**
- Debt‑to‑GDP > 100%
- Ageing population
- Low growth, low inflation
- Strong institutional capacity (statistical agencies, capital markets)
- Large domestic investor base (pension funds, insurance companies)

**Pathway:**

| Phase | Years | Actions |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Pilot** | 1–3 | Issue $1–5 billion of 10‑year Productivity Bonds; establish NPI governance; pass enabling legislation |
| **Expansion** | 4–10 | Increase to 10–15% of new issuance; extend maturities to 20 and 30 years; market to pension funds as a liability‑hedging tool |
| **Integration** | 11–20 | Target 30% of the debt portfolio; integrate into sovereign debt management framework; link to pension fund mandates and insurance regulations |
| **Transformation** | 21+ | Full integration; Productivity Dividend funded from AI royalties and productivity taxes; contribute to global reserve asset discussion |

**Key Enablers:**
- Existing high‑quality statistical infrastructure
- Deep, liquid capital markets
- Strong institutional credibility
- Large pool of long‑term investors seeking inflation‑hedged assets

**Key Risks:**
- **Investor familiarity** – the instrument is new; requires extensive education and roadshows
- **Measurement risk** – the NPI must be seen as credible and independent
- **Political interference** – the NPI methodology must be legally locked to prevent manipulation
- **Feedback loop** – without a principal‑adjustment mechanism, distress could increase in good years

**Critical Success Factor:** Government capture of AI gains (θ_AI) must exceed 30% for the bond to substantially outperform conventional debt.

## Scenario B: Emerging Economy with Growth Potential

**Example countries:** India, Brazil, Indonesia, South Africa

**Profile:**
- Debt‑to‑GDP 60–80%
- Young population
- High growth, moderate inflation
- Developing institutional capacity
- Significant infrastructure and education needs

**Pathway:**

| Phase | Years | Actions |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Pilot** | 1–4 | Partner with a multilateral development bank (e.g., World Bank, IMF) to guarantee or co‑issue the first Productivity Bond; keep issuance small ($500 million – $1 billion) |
| **Expansion** | 5–12 | Link NPI to existing productivity data; issue 5‑ and 10‑year bonds to attract foreign direct investment; use proceeds for productivity‑enhancing infrastructure (energy, transport, digital) |
| **Integration** | 13–20 | Scale to 20% of debt portfolio; link to domestic pension funds; establish a national Productivity Dividend pilot for low‑income citizens |
| **Transformation** | 21+ | Full integration; regional coordination with neighbouring countries to develop a Regional Productivity Index; attract sovereign wealth funds |

**Key Enablers:**
- Technical assistance from IMF, World Bank, or bilateral partners
- Capacity building in national statistical agencies
- Foreign direct investment in productivity‑enhancing sectors
- Strong political will to reform and modernise

**Key Risks:**
- **Data quality and manipulation** – weaker statistical infrastructure increases measurement risk
- **Currency volatility** – issue in local currency with inflation hedge; consider dual‑currency or SDR‑linked variants
- **Political instability** – regime change could threaten the NPI governance framework
- **Investor trust** – requires credible signalling and possibly a multilateral guarantee for early issuances

**Critical Success Factor:** Building an independent, credible NPI governance structure from the outset is essential. A multilateral guarantee can help bridge the trust gap in the pilot phase.

## Scenario C: Small Open Economy with Strong Institutions

**Example countries:** Singapore, New Zealand, Switzerland, Asteria (the book's hypothetical case)

**Profile:**
- Debt‑to‑GDP < 60%
- High institutional quality
- Trade‑dependent, stable growth
- Strong statistical agencies and trusted governance
- Limited domestic investor base (rely on international investors)

**Pathway:**

| Phase | Years | Actions |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Pilot** | 1–2 | Issue a 10‑year Productivity Bond as a demonstration project ($1 billion); use the Asteria model prospectus; position as a global showcase |
| **Expansion** | 3–8 | Increase to 20% of the debt portfolio; market to sovereign wealth funds, global pension funds, and central banks as a reserve asset candidate |
| **Integration** | 9–15 | Full integration into the debt management framework; Productivity Dividend as a national dividend for all citizens; publish annual NPI reports |
| **Transformation** | 16+ | Export the model regionally; contribute to the development of a Global Productivity Index (GPI); host international conferences on productivity‑linked finance |

**Key Enablers:**
- Existing high‑quality data infrastructure
- Trusted institutions and low corruption
- Strong investor base with global reach
- Ability to act as a first‑mover and set global standards

**Key Risks:**
- **Limited domestic investor base** – reliance on international investors increases exposure to global sentiment and capital flows
- **Exchange rate risk** – issue in domestic currency with a hedge; consider issuing dual‑currency bonds
- **First‑mover disadvantage** – all the scrutiny, all the learning costs; but also the opportunity to shape the global narrative

**Critical Success Factor:** This scenario is the lowest‑risk and highest‑reward. A successful pilot here can catalyse global adoption. The key is transparency, communication, and building a coalition of like‑minded countries.

## Cross‑Cutting Success Factors

Across all three scenarios, the following factors are essential:

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Independent NPI Governance** | An independent board, legal lock on methodology, annual audit, and binding dispute resolution |
| **Gradual Transition** | Start small (pilot), scale over decades – 1% → 10% → 30% |
| **Investor Education** | Clear, transparent marketing; prospectus; roadshows; FAQ documents |
| **Productivity Dividend** | Builds public support, maintains the circular flow, reduces inequality |
| **International Coordination** | A Global Productivity Index (GPI) and productivity‑linked SDRs would create a global reserve asset |
| **Principal‑Adjustment Mechanism** | To break the feedback loop and achieve true countercyclical debt service |

---

## Recommended First Steps for Any Country

1. **Define the NPI** – align with existing statistical frameworks; test with historical data
2. **Pass enabling legislation** – lock the methodology; establish the NPI Board
3. **Engage investors** – roadshows, prospectus, pilot auction
4. **Monitor and refine** – learn from the pilot; adjust the floor, cap, and participation rate
5. **Scale gradually** – 1% → 10% → 30% over 25 years
