Model your sovereign's debt trajectory under conventional vs productivity-linked issuance — with the feedback loop made explicit.

⚠️ Model version note: These results use the endogenous-debt model (v1.1). The book's Appendix C used an older fixed-debt calibration — numbers may differ. See CHANGELOG.
📖 What is the Policy Lab? ▼

The Policy Lab puts the Productivity Bond Model in the hands of a sovereign finance analyst. It answers the question every finance ministry asks: "If we swap part of our issuance to Productivity Bonds, what happens to our debt trajectory, our debt service, and our risk of fiscal distress over the next decade?"

Unlike the homepage simulator (which shows summary metrics only), the Policy Lab returns year-by-year debt/GDP and debt service ratios — so you can see when pressure peaks, not just whether it does.

The feedback loop is explicit. Higher coupons in good years increase debt service; if the government borrows to pay, the debt stock grows; when a downturn arrives, the coupon falls but the debt is bigger. This is the model's most important design problem — and the reason for the PAM toggle (Principal Adjustment Mechanism).

❓ FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about the Policy Lab and its results.

🏛️ What does the Policy Lab do? ▼

The Policy Lab is a decision-support tool for sovereign finance ministries and researchers. It simulates the debt trajectory of a country issuing a mix of conventional and productivity-linked bonds.

You get:

  • Year-by-year debt/GDP trajectory — conventional vs productivity
  • Debt service / revenue ratio — with the 15% distress threshold visible
  • Expected cost — the bond price (PV of coupons + principal, risk-adjusted)
  • Distress probability — fraction of paths where DSR exceeds 15% in any year
  • Recommended portfolio share — optimal mix from welfare minimization
  • PAM effect — see what happens when you enable Principal Adjustment
⚙️ What is the PAM toggle? ▼

PAM stands for Principal-Adjustment Mechanism. The simulation shows that variable coupons can create a feedback loop: higher coupons during productive years increase debt service, which increases the debt stock, which increases future debt service — even if the coupon later falls.

The PAM is a rule that automatically reduces outstanding principal when DSR exceeds a threshold (default 15%). When you turn it on, you'll see distress probability drop significantly — at the cost of a small hit to investor returns.

This is the model's most important design refinement. Without PAM, variable coupons may not reduce distress even though they reduce expected cost.

🎯 What is θ_AI and why does it matter? ▼

θ_AI is the fraction of AI-driven productivity gains that the government captures through taxes, royalties, or sovereign equity. It's the single most important parameter in the model.

The empirical evidence is clear:

  • If θ_AI < 0.15, the Productivity Bond barely outperforms conventional debt
  • If θ_AI > 0.30, it substantially outperforms

This is why the verdict warns you when θ_AI is too low — the instrument only works if the gains it's linked to actually reach the public balance sheet.

📊 How do the results compare to the book's Appendix C? ▼

The book's Appendix C used a fixed-debt model (debt path was assumed, not computed). The current Python engine — and this Policy Lab — uses the endogenous-debt model, where each bond type gets its own debt path based on its own coupons.

As a result:

  • Expected costs match closely (0.5154 vs 0.515392 for conventional)
  • Distress probabilities differ (11.05% vs 11.05% for conventional — same; but 88.17% vs 85.68% for productivity)
  • Portfolio optimisation produces a slightly different optimal share (30% in both, but welfare is more nuanced)

The endogenous model is more realistic. The book will be updated in a future edition.

🔧 Is the Policy Lab open source? ▼

Yes. The full engine mirrors run_simulation.py, bond_valuation.py, and state_generator.py. The source code is available on GitHub, and you can run the Python version to verify every number.
