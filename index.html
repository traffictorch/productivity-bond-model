📄 Policy Brief

The Problem in One Sentence

We have built a financial system that assumes unlimited growth on a planet with limits.

The Contradiction

Global debt has grown from ~$50 trillion in 1980 to over $300 trillion today – an almost perfect exponential curve. Global resource extraction is flattening or declining in per‑capita terms. The gap between these two curves is the infinite debt problem.

Why This Matters

Every bond, mortgage, and pension fund assumes tomorrow’s economy will be larger than today’s. This is not a law of nature – it was a historical anomaly made possible by fossil fuels and population growth. Both tailwinds are now headwinds.

The Solution: Productivity Bonds

Instead of a fixed coupon that compounds regardless of performance, a Productivity Bond offers a coupon linked to measurable improvements in productivity.

c(t) = (1 + π) × (1 + min[max(α × g_NPI, 0%), 5%]) – 1
  • Inflation protection – CPI‑linked
  • Productivity participation – 70% of NPI growth
  • Downside protection – 0% real floor
  • Upside limit – 5% real cap

The National Productivity Index (NPI)

Measures GDP per unit of labour, energy, materials, and capital:

NPI = GDP / (Hours^0.40 × Energy^0.25 × Materials^0.20 × Capital^0.15)

Transparent, auditable, and legally locked to prevent manipulation.

Simulation Results (10,000 paths)

  • Cost Saving: −3.1% vs conventional debt
  • Optimal Portfolio: 30% Productivity Bonds / 70% conventional
  • Critical Variable: θ_AI > 0.30 (Government capture of AI gains)

The Productivity Dividend

A universal, unconditional dividend distributed to every citizen:

Dividend(t) = τ × Productivity_Revenue(t) / Population(t)

Funded by AI royalties, productivity taxes, and sovereign AI equity. Reduces inequality and maintains the circular flow.

Transition Pathway

  • Pilot (1–5 yrs): <1% of debt
  • Expansion (6–15 yrs): 5–15% of debt
  • Integration (16–25 yrs): 15–30% of debt
  • Transformation (26+ yrs): 30–50% of debt
